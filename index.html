Aerospace Engineering

History

Aerospace engineering is the design and development of aircraft. It includes aeronautical engineering, which involves aircraft within the Earth's atmosphere, as well as astronautical engineering, which involves aircraft in outer space. The history of aerospace engineering begins with the field of theoretical physics known as astrodynamics. A famous pioneer of this field is Leonardo da Vinci, who illustrated some of the earliest concepts of flight mechanisms. He first concept was the ornithopter, which had wings to mimic the biomechanics of birds. He also conceptualized the aerial screw, which uses a rotor to spin a spiral and generate lift.

In 1783, brothers Joseph-Michel and Jacques-Étienne Montgolfier designed the hot-air balloon and became the first to achieve manned flight. In 1799, Sir George Cayley established the foundational design for modern planes, which included fixed wings and external propulsion system(s). For this reason, Cayley is known as the father of aviation. In 1905, Orville and Wilbur Wright built the first practical airplane and set the groundwork for modern flight.
