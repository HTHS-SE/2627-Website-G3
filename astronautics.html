Astronautics

What is Astronautics?

Astronautics vs. Aeronautics

  • Astronautics is the science and technology behind designing, building, and operating spacecraft.
  • Focuses on humanity's exploration and utilization of space beyond Earth's atmosphere.
  • There are many different areas of astronautics, including the science of spacecraft propulsion and bioastronautics.

Major Milestones

1957 - Sputnik 1

World's first artifical satellite, launched into Earth orbit by the Soviet union.

1963 - Vostok 6

Soviet space mission that carried Valentina Tereshkova into orbit, becoming the first woman in space.

1969 - Apollo 11

Historic American spaceflight that brought the first ever humans to the Moon from Kennedy Space Center.

2000 - ISS Station

A large habitable satellite orbiting Earth, jointly operated by five international space agencies.

Pioneers of Astronautics

Konstantin Tsiolkovsky

  • Widely acknowledged as the founding father of modern rocketry and astronautics.
  • Derived the Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation, relating change in velocity to exhaust velocity.
  • First conceived the idea of using liquid-fueled rockets for space travel.
  • Build the first aerodynamic wind tunnel in Russia, 1897.

Robbert H. Goddard

  • Known for launching the world's first liquid-propellant rocket in 1926.
  • Used mathematics to show that rockets could work in a vacuum.
  • Also launched the first rocket containing a barometer and camera in 1929.
  • NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center is named in his honor.

Hermann Oberth

  • Discovered the Oberth effect, describing the kinetic energy of a rocket engine.
  • Showed that multistage rockets were essential for a vehicle to achieve escape velocity.
  • Published "The Rocket into Interplanetary Space" in 1923, an incredibly influential self-published thesis.
  • Won the Robert Esnault-Pelterie Prize for his contributions.
